Page 04 · Design notes

Build the business behind the bookings.

One page, two entry points: Launch (0–1 units, free webinar) and Scale (several units, strategy call). The page's job is to help each visitor pick the right one and take that step.

1 Audience & why two paths

Launch is for employed people with no units or one unit who want an income-generating business of their own. It's the larger audience and the cheaper entry offer, so it gets the primary visual weight: sage CTA, first position, and a low-commitment free webinar.

Scale is for operators with several units who are held back by manual work, thin margins and no time. It's the premium offer, so it gets a darker, premium treatment (forest and brass), a full value proposition and equal space in every decision section. It is never tucked into a dropdown or the footer.

The two groups have different problems, so the page names both problems separately before it shows any offer.

2 The Flex · Base360 · Flex Academy

The page tells one story in three steps: Operate → Systemise → Teach. The Flex brings hospitality, warmth and architectural photography (warm interior imagery, sand tones). Base360 brings systems and operational clarity (a product UI card and the 130+ channel fact). Flex Academy uses a restrained editorial system that sits between the two, so it reads as a sibling brand rather than a copy of either.

Neither parent logo was redesigned. Flex Academy uses a provisional wordmark with an endorsement line, "By the founders of The Flex".

3 Architecture & conversion logic

Nav (both CTAs)→Hero: two doors→Proof strip→Two problems→Choose your path ★→Method→Founders→Compare→Outcomes (honest slot)→Who it's for + unit selector→FAQ→Final CTA + checklist→Footer

The first screen answers what the Academy is, who it's for and what to do next. Proof comes straight after the hero because the main objection is "why trust these people?" The problem section helps visitors recognise their stage before they see offers. The founders and comparison sections handle the objections a visitor has after choosing a path. Every scroll depth has a contextual next step, but no section repeats a CTA without a reason. Each CTA sits beside the specific promise it acts on.

4 Three key design decisions

A "two-door" hero, not a hero plus pricing cards

The hero splits one panel into Launch (sage, primary, larger audience) and Scale (dark, premium). Visitors choose their stage in the first five seconds.

Why: a single CTA would make Scale an afterthought, and two pricing cards would feel like a SaaS funnel. One panel keeps both paths visibly part of one academy.

A colour code that means something

Sage = Launch and the primary action. Forest and brass = Scale and premium. The same code runs through the badges, path panels, comparison columns, dialogs and confirmations.

Why: once learned, it lets visitors scan the page for "their" content, and the extra cues reinforce self-selection. Status is never shown by colour alone: labels and icons always accompany it.

Honest placeholders as a design element

Prices X and Y, the webinar date, time slots, budget bands, bios and testimonials all use one dashed orange placeholder style. They're visible in the design, but the layout doesn't break when real content arrives.

Why: credibility is the product. An unverified number would undermine the one thing the page needs visitors to believe.

5 Trade-off / assumption

Mobile path switcher. On mobile, Launch and Scale sit behind a segmented control instead of being stacked, which roughly halves the scroll length. The risk is that Scale content is hidden by default. To mitigate this, the tab labels show both audiences, the Scale tab restyles in forest when selected, a "View Scale" hint follows the panel, and Scale stays reachable from the hero, the sticky bar, the comparison rows and the final CTA.

Assumption: "several units" means 2 or more. The unit selector routes 0–1 → Launch and 2+ → Scale.

6 First experiment after launch

Hero A/B test: the two-door hero (control) against a single primary webinar CTA with a text-link Scale route (variant).

Primary metric: total qualified conversions (webinar registrations + booked calls). Guardrail: Scale call bookings must not drop, and neither should the share of callers who meet the "several units" criterion.

This tests the page's biggest bet: whether showing both paths up front helps self-selection or splits attention.

The forest-and-sage direction comes from the supplied reference layout. All pairings pass WCAG AA: ink on sage is 13:1, and body text is muted #59625C on paper at 5.9:1. Radii are 10, 16, 24 and 32; spacing uses a 4/8 base; the grid is 12 columns with a 1200px container; mobile has 20px margins, 44px+ touch targets and 16px inputs, so iOS doesn't zoom on focus.

Page 04 · Design notes

Build the business behind the bookings.

One page, two entry points: Launch (0–1 units, free webinar) and Scale (several units, strategy call). The page's job is to help each visitor pick the right one and take that step.

1 Audience & why two paths

Launch is for employed people with no units or one unit who want an income-generating business of their own. It's the larger audience and the cheaper entry offer, so it gets the primary visual weight: sage CTA, first position, and a low-commitment free webinar.

Scale is for operators with several units who are held back by manual work, thin margins and no time. It's the premium offer, so it gets a darker, premium treatment (forest and brass), a full value proposition and equal space in every decision section. It is never tucked into a dropdown or the footer.

The two groups have different problems, so the page names both problems separately before it shows any offer.

2 The Flex · Base360 · Flex Academy

The page tells one story in three steps: Operate → Systemise → Teach. The Flex brings hospitality and real photography (apartment image in the Launch section, city pins). Base360 brings systems and operational clarity (the method cards, the 130+ channel fact). Flex Academy sits on the supplied reference's charcoal / white / green system so it reads as a sibling brand rather than a copy of either parent.

Neither parent logo was redesigned. Flex Academy uses a provisional wordmark with an endorsement line, "By the founders of The Flex".

3 Architecture & conversion logic

Nav (both CTAs)→Hero: email pill + Scale link→Method (6 cards)→Angled proof strip→Launch ★ (split + circle)→Scale ★ (dark section)→What you get (grid)→Why not the alternatives→Founders→Who it's for + unit selector→Outcomes (honest slots)→FAQ→Final CTA + footer + checklist

The first screen answers what the Academy is, who it's for and what to do next: the email pill is the low-friction Launch entry (it pre-fills the registration form), and the Scale link sits directly beneath it so operators aren't sent through the wrong funnel. The method cards and proof strip establish credibility before the offers. Launch and Scale then each get a full section with its own problem framing, components, price placeholder and CTA. The founders, qualification and FAQ sections handle the objections a visitor has after choosing a path.

4 Three key design decisions

Email pill for Launch, text link for Scale — in the same hero

The reference's single email pill maps to the larger Launch audience: one field, one green button. Scale gets an explicit underlined link one line below, plus a permanent nav button.

Why: the pill keeps the first action to a single field, while the Scale link means existing operators never have to hunt — and never register for a beginner webinar by mistake.

Light section = Launch, dark section = Scale

The reference alternates white and charcoal blocks. Launch takes the white split section with the circular image; Scale takes the dark section with the circular portrait. Green is reserved for the primary action and Launch tags.

Why: the alternation the reference already has becomes meaning — visitors can tell which programme a section is about before reading it. Status is never shown by colour alone.

Honest placeholders as a design element

Prices X and Y, the webinar date, time slots, budget bands, bios and testimonials all use one dashed orange placeholder style. They're visible in the design, but the layout doesn't break when real content arrives.

Why: credibility is the product. An unverified number would undermine the one thing the page needs visitors to believe.

5 Trade-off / assumption

One primary action in the hero. Following the reference, the hero has a single email pill. That's right for the larger Launch audience but gives Scale less first-screen weight than a two-button hero would. Mitigation: the Scale link sits directly under the pill, a Scale button is fixed in the nav and the mobile sticky bar, and Scale owns the page's only dark feature section.

Assumption: "several units" means 2 or more. The unit selector routes 0–1 → Launch and 2+ → Scale.

6 First experiment after launch

Hero A/B test: the email pill hero (control) against a two-button hero with equal Launch / Scale CTAs and no email field (variant).

Primary metric: total qualified conversions (webinar registrations + booked calls). Guardrail: Scale call bookings must not drop, and neither should the share of callers who meet the "several units" criterion.

This tests the page's biggest bet: whether a single-field pill converts more beginners than it costs in Scale bookings.

8 Proposed offer structure — recommendation, not confirmed offer

Launch: add a fixed start and a finish line

Confirmed: self-paced course, live group sessions, templates. Recommended: run the live sessions as a 4–6 week cohort with a named "first unit live" milestone, so the offer has a date, not just content.

Why: the 9-to-5 leaver buys momentum. A deadline also gives the webinar a concrete thing to close towards.

Scale: lead with the founder 1:1 and Base360

Confirmed: 12 weeks, weekly group, monthly founder 1:1, community, SOPs, Base360 access. Recommended: present it as "operating your business on The Flex's stack for 12 weeks" and state clearly what happens to Base360 access at the end.

Why: for an early operator the software and founder time are the two things YouTube and masterminds can't offer; the end-of-programme terms are the first objection on the call.

Bridge: a Launch → Scale path

Recommended: a stated upgrade route (Launch fee credited against Scale, or a Launch-graduate cohort) so the two offers read as one journey.

Why: it makes the "one academy, two entry points" story literal and gives Launch graduates a next step without a second cold sale.

Loom outline (≤ 5 min)

  1. 0:00 Who it's for. Two people, two offers. The 9-to-5 leaver (Launch) is the bigger market and leads: primary pill in the hero, first section after the strip. The early operator (Scale) is premium: the page's only dark feature section, a permanent nav button, the link under the hero pill.
  2. 0:45 Structure. Hero → method → proof strip → Launch → Scale → what you get → alternatives → founders → who it's for → outcomes → FAQ → final CTA + footer. Trust before offers; each offer with its own problem, components, price placeholder and CTA; act at any scroll depth.
  3. 1:45 Three brands, one story. Operate (The Flex) → Systemise (Base360) → Teach (Academy). Sibling, not copy: the reference's charcoal / white / green system, real facts only.
  4. 2:30 Three decisions. (1) Email pill for Launch + text link for Scale in one hero. (2) Light = Launch, dark = Scale, carried through the page. (3) Honest placeholders as a visible design element — no invented prices, dates, reviews or photos.
  5. 3:45 Flows. Launch: 3 fields → saving → confirmed with add-to-calendar. Scale: 4-question qualifier → calendar + slot + time zone → booked. Secondary: waitlist / checklist.
  6. 4:30 First test. Pill hero vs two-button hero; metric = total qualified conversions, guardrail = Scale bookings don't drop.

The charcoal / white / green system is taken from the supplied reference. All pairings pass WCAG AA: ink on green is 9.0:1, muted text #6A716D on white is 5.0:1, and #B6BDB9 on charcoal is 7.0:1. Radii are 8, 12, 16 and 20; spacing uses a 4/8 base; the grid is 12 columns with an 1180px container; mobile has 20px margins, 44px+ touch targets and 16px inputs, so iOS doesn't zoom on focus.
